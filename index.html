How to use this worksheet

    

      
This worksheet walks you through appraising a research paper section by section, then hands the result off to your reference manager. Here's the whole workflow.


      
1Fill it out

      
    
        
  • Paste a paper's DOI at the top and click Fetch citation to auto-fill the journal, year, authors, and citation. Every field stays editable.
    • 
        
  • Work down the numbered sections — click a header to expand it. Its dot turns bronze when you start it and gold when it's complete; the bar up top tracks overall progress.
    • 
        
  • Hover the ? icons for definitions (for example, each internal-validity threat).
    • 
        
  • The Identification Strategy section stays locked until you indicate the authors make a causal claim — that's intentional: you only interrogate an identification strategy when a causal claim is on the table.
    • 
      


      
2How saving works

      
Everything you type autosaves automatically — but only in this web browser on this computer. There's nothing to click day-to-day.

      
    
        
  • Multiple papers: use the Worksheet dropdown and ＋ New to keep a separate worksheet for each paper, and switch between them anytime.
    • 
        
  • Autosave won't follow you to another browser or computer, and it can be erased by a private/incognito window or by clearing your browsing data.
    • 
      

      
✅ The habit to build: when you finish a paper, click Export → JSON → Download. That file is your durable, portable copy. To resume later — on any device — click Import JSON and pick that file.


      
3Attach it to your reference manager

      
The completed worksheet can travel with the paper in Paperpile, Zotero, or Mendeley.

      
If the paper isn't in your library yet:

      
    
        
  1. Click Export → RIS → Download.
    2. 
        
  2. Import that .ris file into your reference manager.
    3. 
        
  3. You get a reference entry with the full worksheet in its Notes and your key answers (field, study design, causal claim, flagged threats) as tags.
    4. 
      

      
If the paper is already in your library (importing RIS would create a duplicate):

      
    
        
  1. Click Export → Markdown → Copy.
    2. 
        
  2. Open the existing reference and paste into its Notes field.
    3. 
      

      
In Zotero, the RIS note imports as a child note and the tags attach to the item. In Paperpile and Mendeley, the worksheet lands in the reference's Notes and the keywords import as labels/tags.
